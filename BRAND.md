## Company name: deferred (2026-11-11)

"Kept" was chosen, checked, and dropped. It is **blocked**:

| Field | Value |
| --- | --- |
| Mark | KEPT (word mark) |
| Serial | 50171161 |
| Status | Live, pending |
| Classes | IC 009, IC 041, IC 042 |
| Filed | 2026-10-20 |
| Owner | Janus AI Solutions LLC, Raleigh, NC |

Identical word mark, the two classes that matter, filed first. Nineteen other
live marks in classes 9 and 42 contain "kept". Evidence and reasoning:
`docs/brand-check.md`.

**Decision: ship the software as AISL and defer the company name.** A company
name is required at incorporation, at a signed agreement, and at the first
payment. None of those is imminent, and none of them is a prerequisite for
publishing.

AISL is the product name, the CLI, and the npm package. It is descriptive and
therefore weak as a trademark, which is normal and acceptable for infrastructure.
The company name gets chosen later, when it has to be real.

### The deferral got more expensive (2026-11-12)

Still deferred, but no longer free to defer. Two facts changed the cost:

1. **The revenue path starts sooner.** The recommended sequence leads with a
   product that ships now (`docs/portfolio-wedge.md`), so the first invoice, and
   therefore the entity, arrives earlier than "when it has to be real" implied.
2. **The name is a distribution asset, not just a legal one.** The open-source
   route to attention runs through a GitHub org, a Product Hunt launch, the
   open-source credit programs, and "the team you want support from." All of
   those are the company mark. The slot is now on the critical path.

The naming method also fixes the structural mistake behind the KEPT loss: one
name was asked to be both the registerable company mark and the descriptive
product mark. See `docs/naming-method.md`. Legal screening comes first, creative
work happens inside the cleared space, and the two names keep separate jobs.

Background on the business consequences: `docs/business-and-ip.md`.

## Copy discipline

One hero line. Each line has exactly one home. The failure mode is stacking all
three in the hero block, which reads as noise — big brands run **one** line for a
decade (Mastercard "Priceless" since 1997, CrowdStrike "We stop breaches" since
2011).

| Surface | Line |
| --- | --- |
| Homepage hero, deck, footer | Nothing leaves without a record. |
| Logo lockup | Held in. Accounted for. |
| Comparison page, sales call | The fence, not the guardrail. |

The tagline never repeats the company name. A name that restates the promise
makes the sentence eat itself.

## Why "Kept" was chosen, and why it was dropped

Kept as a record of the reasoning, not as a recommendation. Do not revive it.

Why it was chosen:

- It is the past participle of the exact verb the product performs: data is
  **held**, **maintained**, and **not released**.
- It is one syllable, pronounceable in every major language, and has no
  unfortunate rhyme.
- It carries the containment sense (kept in), the care sense (well-kept), and the
  confidentiality sense (a well-kept secret): three meanings, one word.
- The archaic "kept man" reading is a dictionary footnote, not a live mental
  category.

Why it was dropped: an identical live word mark in classes 9 and 42, serial
50171161, filed 2026-10-20. See the deferred-name section above and
`docs/brand-check.md`.

## Availability facts

Checked 2026-10-27 → 2026-11-01 across GitHub orgs, npm, and `.com`/`.io`/`.dev`:

**Every single real English word tested was taken on all four.** Roughly twenty
names, including `kept`, `held`, `bound`, `berth`, `trove`, `redoubt`,
`holdfast`, `safekeep`, `donjon`, `coffer`, `holt`, `fafnir`, plus Greek, Hebrew,
Finnish and Japanese words for guardian. A meaningful, unmodified, free name is
an empty set — the namespace land rush ended around 2015.

What survived was always either a compound or a coinage (`aisl`, `nemion`).
