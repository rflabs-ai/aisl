# Pilot targets — 50 organizations

Ranked by **likelihood of replying**, not by likelihood of buying. Those are not
the same list, and mixing them is how outreach dies: the people who answer fast
are rarely the people who sign.

- **Tier 1 — likely to reply.** AI-native or AI-forward teams who are actively
  being asked the question by *their own* customers. Short sales cycle, small
  budget, high signal.
- **Tier 2 — may reply.** Mid-market regulated firms with a named security
  leader and a live agent pilot.
- **Tier 3 — unlikely to reply, worth ten of the others.** Large enterprises.
  Slow, but one reply is a real conversation.

> These are category targets drawn from public knowledge, not confirmed buyers.
> Verify the company still exists in that form, find the current person, and
> prefer a warm path over a cold one. A list of 50 is a pool; send **20**.

## How to reach them

Prefer, in order:

1. **Warm intro** — anyone you or a YC contact knows at the company.
2. **A public signal** — they wrote about agents, spoke at a conference, or
   shipped something. Reply to the *thing they said*, not to the company.
3. **LinkedIn** — search `<company> CISO`, `<company> head of security`,
   `<company> security engineering manager`. One paragraph, no attachment.
4. **Cold email** — last resort, lowest rate.

## The message (three sentences, no attachment)

> You've got agents touching <their data type>. The question you'll be asked is
> how you prove what they did. I built an enforcement plane that answers it —
> signed, chained, anchored evidence per action. Six-week pilot on one workflow,
> $4k for the first two partners. Worth 20 minutes?

## What to track

One row per contact: company, person, path (warm/cold), sent date, reply,
next step. Twenty sends, then read the replies before sending twenty more —
the second batch should use the message the first batch taught you.

**A reply that says "we can't give you a sandbox" is not a no.** It is a product
requirement: host a demo environment.
